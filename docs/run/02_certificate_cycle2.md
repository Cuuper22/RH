# Certificate architect, cycle 2: the prime-pair transfer

## Executive result

The requested implication from an *almost-all, logarithmic-accuracy* Hardy--Littlewood statement is false as stated.  The exact endpoint kernel shows why: after the apparent (1/\log(n/m)) singularity cancels, every near-diagonal shift has weight of order (T L).  An exceptional set of size (H\log^{-A}X), or errors of relative size (\log^{-A}X) on (H) good shifts, can therefore swamp the entire (T L^3) second-moment scale by the polynomial factor (H).

What does transfer is either:

1. a uniform power-saving prime-pair error (Y^{1-\delta}) with
   
   \[
   \delta>1-\lambda^{-1}=\frac{43}{143}=0.300699\ldots,
   \tag{1}
   \]

   together with the same power control on the exceptional mass; or

2. the directly weighted aggregate discrepancy (18) below.

Under either repaired input, the transfer is complete and gives the exact constant from cycle 1,

\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(B_T^2)}{N(T,2T)}
=\frac{2561811364469143}{2227707598259143}+o(1)
=1.1499764899\ldots<\frac{23}{20},
\tag{2}
\]

and therefore

\[
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T,2T)}{N(T,2T)}
\ge0.8500235101\ldots>0.85.
\tag{3}
\]

The exponent comparison (43/143>8/33) is favorable only for the *length of the top shift interval*.  It does not supply the power accuracy needed after cancellation, and on lower dyadic prime ranges the (8/33) theorem applies only to the sub-band (\log n/\log T\ge33/25=1.32).

## 1. Exact off-diagonal kernel

Retain the paper's notation

\[
L=\lambda\log(T/2\pi),\qquad X=e^L=T^{\lambda+o(1)},\qquad
a_n=\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n},
\]

and let (\Phi) be the Fourier kernel generated by the chosen window.  Proposition 5.6 defines

\[
\alpha_n^+=\int_0^T\Phi(x)^2n^{ix}\,dx,\qquad
\alpha_n^-=\int_{-T}^0\Phi(x)^2n^{ix}\,dx.
\tag{4}
\]

Its displayed formula for (O_1), after joining the two orientations (m>n) and (n>m), is

\[
O_1=\frac1{\pi^2}\Re
\sum_{h\ge1}\sum_{n+h\le X}
\frac{\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)}{\sqrt{n(n+h)}}\,
\mathcal K_T(n,h),
\tag{5}
\]

where, with (\delta=\log(1+h/n)>0),

\[
\mathcal K_T(n,h)
=\frac{
e^{-2iT\delta}(\alpha_{n+h}^++\alpha_n^-)
-e^{-iT\delta}(\alpha_n^++\alpha_{n+h}^-)
}{-i\delta}.
\tag{6}
\]

This formula tracks both endpoint phases and the (1/\log(n/m)) factor exactly.

The apparent singularity at (h=0) is removable.  Indeed,

\[
\frac{e^{-2iT\delta}-e^{-iT\delta}}{\delta}
=-2i e^{-3iT\delta/2}\frac{\sin(T\delta/2)}{\delta},
\tag{7}
\]

so its magnitude is at most (\min(T,2/\delta)).  Also

\[
\alpha_n^++\alpha_n^-=2\pi g(\log n),qquad
\left|\frac{d}{d\log n}\alpha_n^\pm\right|
\le\int_{\mathbb R}|x|\Phi(x)^2\,dx\ll\log L.
\tag{8}
\]

Adding and subtracting (\alpha_n^\pm) in (6), and using (\delta\asymp h/n) for (h\le n), gives

\[
|\mathcal K_T(n,h)|
\ll L\min\!\left(T,\frac{n}{h}\right)+\log L.
\tag{9}
\]

On a smooth dyadic block (n\asymp Y), the complete coefficient multiplying (\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)) therefore obeys

\[
|\mathcal W_{Y,h}(n)|
\ll \frac{L}{Y}\min\!\left(T,\frac{Y}{h}\right)+\frac{\log L}{Y}.
\tag{10}
\]

The derivatives needed for smooth weighted prime-pair estimates follow directly from

\[
\partial_n^r e^{-ijT\log(1+h/n)}
\ll_r Y^{-r}\left(1+\frac{Th}{Y}\right)^r,
\tag{11}
\]

and the corresponding derivatives of (4).  Thus the weights are ordinary fixed-complexity smooth weights when (h\lesssim Y/T); for larger (h), (11) records exactly the oscillation that a weighted hypothesis must preserve.

## 2. Dyadic correlation discrepancy

Let (w) range over a fixed smooth dyadic partition of unity.  For every weight (W=W_{T,Y,h}) obtained from (6), the cutoff (w(n/Y)), and a bounded number of integrations by parts, define

\[
\Delta_Y(h;W)
:=
\sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)W(n/Y)
-\mathfrak S(h)Y\int_0^\infty W(u)\,du.
\tag{12}
\]

Here (\mathfrak S(h)) is the prime-pair singular series.  The von Mangoldt formulation already includes prime powers, so no deletion of prime powers is needed when applying an input stated for (\Lambda).

Substitution of (12) in (5), followed by the same decomposition for the mixed (M[\mu,P_X]) term, splits the second moment into

\[
M=M_{\rm HL}+M_{\rm err}+o(TL^3),
\qquad M=\operatorname{tr}\widetilde G^{2},
\tag{13}
\]

with

\[
|M_{\rm err}|
\ll
\sum_{Y\ {m dyadic}\le X}\sum_{h}
\left(\frac{L}{Y}\min\!\left(T,\frac{Y}{h}\right)+\frac{\log L}{Y}\right)
|\Delta_Y(h;W_{T,Y,h})|.
\tag{14}
\]

For pairs in noncomparable dyadic blocks the same statement holds with (Y) replaced by (\sqrt{Y_1Y_2}) in the denominator and the exact phase from (6).  Equivalently, one may use overlapping blocks with (n\asymp n+h\) for (14) and include the finitely many two-scale weights in the family (W).  This prevents a hidden appeal to an unweighted or nonuniform correlation estimate.

The main term (M_{\rm HL}) is obtained by replacing the correlation with (\mathfrak S(h)).  Inserting the Ramanujan expansion

\[
\mathfrak S(h)=\sum_{q\ge1}\frac{\mu(q)^2}{\varphi(q)^2}c_q(h)
\tag{15}
\]

and applying Poisson summation to the smooth (h)-weights in (6) gives the usual pair-correlation density (F(\alpha)=\min(|\alpha|,1)).  The (q=1) term is the large smooth-density contribution that cancels against (2M[\mu,P_X]+M[\mu,\mu]); retaining it separately is precisely what creates misleading terms of size (LX).  The remaining terms and the diagonal give, for a profile (v),

\[
M_{\rm HL}
=\frac{T L^3}{2\pi\lambda^2}
\left[
\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}v(s)^2\,ds
+\lambda\!\iint_{[-1/2,1/2]^2}
\min(\lambda|s-t|,1)v(s)v(t)\,ds\,dt
\right]
+o(TL^3).
\tag{16}
\]

This calculation is also obtained directly by changing variables (\alpha=\lambda(s-t)) after the Ramanujan sum.  It is the (\lambda>1) extension of the paper's equation (7.2); for (\lambda\le1), the minimum becomes (\lambda|s-t|) and (16) reduces to the accepted formula.

Since

\[
N(T,2T)=\frac{TL}{2\pi\lambda}+o(TL),
\qquad B_T=\frac{\widetilde G}{L\int v},
\]

(16) gives

\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(B_T^2)}N
=
\frac{
\int v^2+\lambda\iint\min(\lambda|s-t|,1)v(s)v(t)
}{\lambda(\int v)^2}+o(1),
\tag{17}
\]

provided (M_{\rm err}=o(TL^3)).

## 3. Correct transfer lemma

**Lemma (weighted Hardy--Littlewood transfer).**  Fix (1<\lambda<2).  Suppose that the correlation discrepancies (12), uniformly for the finite weight family extracted from (6), satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{Y\ {m dyadic}\le X}\sum_h
\left(\frac{L}{Y}\min\!\left(T,\frac{Y}{h}\right)+\frac{\log L}{Y}\right)
|\Delta_Y(h;W_{T,Y,h})|
=o(TL^3).}
\tag{18}
\]

Then (17) holds.  For (\lambda=143/100) and (v(s)=1-(169/100)s^2), equations (2)--(3) follow.

*Proof.*  Equations (5)--(12) are an exact reindexing of Proposition 5.6, with smooth dyadic partitioning.  Bound the discrepancy part with (9), obtaining (14), which is (o(TL^3)) by (18).  Prime powers omitted from a primes-only version contribute

\[
\ll\sum_{Y\le X}Y^{1/2}L^4\ll X^{1/2}L^4=o(TL^3)
\]

because (X^{1/2}=o(T)) for (\lambda<2).  The (O_2), pole, and tails retain power savings for (\lambda<2) after the same smooth decomposition; alternatively they are included among the finite two-scale weights in (18).  Evaluate the singular-series replacement by (15) and Poisson summation, giving (16).  Divide by ((L\int v)^2N) to obtain (17).  The exact rational calculation for the stated (v) is cycle 1, equation (10), and gives (2).  The accepted zero-side inequality then gives (3).  \(\square\)

### A simple power-saving hypothesis that implies (18)

Assume, with no uncontrolled exceptions, that for every required smooth weight

\[
|\Delta_Y(h;W)|\ll Y^{1-\delta}L^{-A}
\tag{19}
\]

uniformly in (Y\le X) and the relevant shifts.  Since

\[
\sum_{h\le Y}\min\!\left(T,\frac{Y}{h}\right)
\ll Y\log T,
\tag{20}
\]

(14) is

\[
M_{\rm err}\ll X^{1-\delta}L^{2-A+O(1)}.
\tag{21}
\]

As (T=X^{1/\lambda+o(1)}), (21) is (o(TL^3)) whenever

\[
\delta>1-\frac1\lambda.
\tag{22}
\]

For (\lambda=143/100), this is exactly (1).  The conjectural square-root Hardy--Littlewood error has (\delta=1/2) and is more than sufficient.

An almost-all version of (19) also transfers if its exceptional shifts satisfy the weighted mass condition

\[
\sum_{Y}\sum_{h\in\mathcal E_Y}
\min\!\left(T,\frac{Y}{h}\right)
\frac{|\Delta_Y(h;W)|}{Y}
=o(TL^2).
\tag{23}
\]

Condition (23), rather than the cardinality of (\mathcal E_Y), is the exact missing datum.

## 4. Why logarithmic-density exceptions do not transfer

Let (H_Y=Y/T), and consider (h\asymp H_Y).  Then (\delta\asymp1/T), so (7)--(10) show that one shift with a correlation discrepancy of natural size (Y) contributes order (TL) to (M_{\rm err}).  If

\[
|\mathcal E_Y|\asymp H_YL^{-A},
\]

the sieve bound (\sum_n\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)w(n/Y)\ll\mathfrak S(h)Y\) permits the exceptional contribution

\[
TL\,|\mathcal E_Y|\asymp TLH_YL^{-A}.
\tag{24}
\]

Relative to the permitted scale (TL^3), (24) is

\[
\frac{H_Y}{L^{A+2}},
\tag{25}
\]

which tends to infinity for every fixed (A) when (H_Y) is a positive power of (Y).  The endpoint phases do not rescue this: place the exceptional shifts consecutively in an interval of length (H_YL^{-A}).  On such an interval (T\log(1+h/n)) changes by only (O(L^{-A})), so all phases in (6) are essentially aligned.  This is a concrete admissible error pattern under an all-but-(H\log^{-A}) assertion, and it violates (18).

The same calculation applies to good-shift errors (Y L^{-A}): summing them over (H_Y) shifts again produces (24).  Thus allowing an arbitrarily large but fixed logarithmic exponent (A) is not a substitute for the power saving (22).

## 5. Exact comparison with the (8/33) theorem

At the top length (Y=X),

\[
H_X=\frac XT=X^{43/143+o(1)},
\qquad
\frac{43}{143}-\frac8{33}=\frac{275}{4719}>0.
\tag{26}
\]

So the top shift interval is indeed longer than the known (X^{8/33+\varepsilon}) threshold.  Two independent gaps remain:

1. That theorem supplies errors (Y\log^{-A}Y) outside (O(H_Y\log^{-A}Y)) exceptions, not (18), (19) with (\delta>43/143), or (23).
2. On a lower dyadic prime range (Y), its condition (Y/T\ge Y^{8/33+\varepsilon}) requires, at the exponent level,
   
   \[
   Y\ge X^{132/143+o(1)}=T^{33/25+o(1)}.
   \tag{27}
   \]
   
   Hence it covers only the frequency sub-band (1.32\le\log Y/\log T\le1.43), while the functional (17) also uses (1<\log Y/\log T<1.32).

The comparison (43/143>8/33) therefore identifies useful arithmetic technology, but it does not by itself close the support-1.43 certificate.

## 6. Next constructive attack

The shortest viable target is no longer a pointwise almost-all theorem.  It is one of:

- prove (18) directly for the single quadratic profile, retaining the endpoint phases so cancellation occurs before absolute values;
- strengthen the relevant shifted-prime estimate to a weighted mean-square bound with effective power saving (\delta>43/143);
- redesign the matrix height envelope to be smooth, making the endpoint Fourier kernel rapidly decreasing, and then prove the resulting analogue of (18) from the minor-arc (L^2) estimate rather than from exceptional-set counting.

The third option is the most plausible use of the (8/33) technology: it attacks the exact Fourier integral from which the almost-all correlation theorem is derived and avoids throwing away its cancellation by first converting it to a list of good and exceptional shifts.

Quantitatively, on a top block the required localized minor-arc estimate has the natural scale

\[
\int_{|\theta-\beta|\ll1/H}|S_\Lambda(\theta)|^2\,d\theta
\ll \frac{X}{H}L^{O(1)},
\tag{28}
\]

uniformly for the frequencies selected by the endpoint kernel.  The published (8/33) minor-arc input gives (\ll X L^{-A}).  This is enough for an almost-all-shifts theorem but is still larger than (28) by (H/L^{A+O(1)}).  Thus the precise new analytic lemma is a power-strengthened local (L^2) estimate, not merely a repackaging of the existing logarithmic-saving estimate.
