# Certificate architect, cycle 1

## Result: a single weighted support-1.43 observable forces more than 85%

Write (N=N(T,2T)), (s=N_{0}^{s}(T,2T)), and use the paper's matrix in the normalization in which an isolated simple on-line zero has eigenvalue (1).  The accepted zero-side inequality is

\[
s\ge 4\operatorname{tr}B_T-2N-\operatorname{tr}(B_T^2)-o(N).
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently the following is already a complete sufficient condition:

\[
\operatorname{tr}B_T=N+o(N),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(B_T^2)\le \left(\frac{23}{20}-\eta+o(1)\right)N
\tag{2}
\]

for any fixed (eta\ge0).  Indeed, (1) gives

\[
s\ge \left(2-\frac{23}{20}+\eta-o(1)\right)N
  =\left(\frac{17}{20}+\eta-o(1)\right)N.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus the weakest new scalar observable is not a general correlation theorem: it is the one-sided second-moment estimate

\[
\boxed{\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(B_T^2)}{N(T,2T)}\le \frac{23}{20}.}
\tag{4}
\]

No higher trace, new matrix inequality, or separate count is required if (4) can be obtained.

### The correct functional past bandwidth one

For a nonnegative profile (v) on ([-1/2,1/2]), bandwidth (lambda>1), and the pair-correlation law (F(\alpha)=\min(|\alpha|,1)), the extension of the paper's functional is

\[
c_{\rm pc}(\lambda,v)
=
\frac{\lambda\left(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}v\right)^2}
{\displaystyle
 \int_{-1/2}^{1/2}v(s)^2\,ds
 +\lambda\!\iint_{[-1/2,1/2]^2}
       \min\!\bigl(\lambda|s-t|,1\bigr)v(s)v(t)\,ds\,dt}.
\tag{5}
\]

For (lambda\le1), (5) reduces exactly to the paper's (7.3), since the minimum is then (lambda|s-t|).  If the single weighted pair-correlation asymptotic associated with (v) holds, the prime side gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}(B_T^2)=\left(c_{\rm pc}(\lambda,v)^{-1}+o(1)\right)N,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}B_T=N+o(N).
\tag{6}
\]

Hence (c_{\rm pc}\ge20/23) is precisely the 85% threshold.

### An exact elementary window at support 1.43

Take

\[
\lambda=\frac{143}{100},\qquad
v(s)=1-\frac{169}{100}s^2\quad (|s|\le1/2).
\tag{7}
\]

This profile is strictly positive.  Put

\[
g(u)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2-u}v(s)v(s+u)\,ds\quad(0\le u\le1).
\]

Direct polynomial integration gives

\[
g(u)=\frac{1809683}{2400000}
-\frac{53361}{160000}u
-\frac{174239}{120000}u^2
+\frac{169}{150}u^3
-\frac{28561}{300000}u^5,
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
A&:=\int v=\frac{1031}{1200},\\
B&:=\int v^2=\frac{1809683}{2400000},\\
J&:=\iint\min(\lambda|s-t|,1)v(s)v(t)\,ds\,dt\\
 &=2\left(\lambda\int_0^{1/\lambda}u g(u)\,du
        +\int_{1/\lambda}^{1}g(u)\,du\right)
 =\frac{970487502160963}{3017889594720000}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Substitution into (5) yields the exact value

\[
c_{\rm pc}\left(\frac{143}{100},v\right)
=\frac{\lambda A^2}{B+\lambda J}
=\frac{2227707598259143}{2561811364469143}
=0.8695829947\ldots
>\frac{20}{23}.
\tag{10}
\]

Therefore (1) and (6) give

\[
\frac{s}{N}
\ge
2-\frac1{c_{\rm pc}}-o(1)
=\frac{1893603832049143}{2227707598259143}-o(1)
=0.8500235101\ldots-o(1).
\tag{11}
\]

The strict margin above (17/20) is

\[
\frac{1047470577429}{44554151965182860}
=2.3510\times10^{-5}.
\tag{12}
\]

Thus a pair-correlation input only through Fourier support (1.43), and only for this one quadratic profile, suffices.  This improves the support-(1.4734269\ldots) benchmark obtained by formally continuing the (|\alpha|) diagonal law past (1).  The saturation (F(\alpha)=1) for (|\alpha|>1) is useful and must be included.  Variationally optimizing (5) gives the equation

\[
\left[I+\lambda T_\lambda\right]v=\text{constant},
\qquad
(T_\lambda v)(s)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\min(\lambda|s-t|,1)v(t)\,dt,
\tag{13}
\]

and places the apparent minimum support near (1.4296); (7)--(12), however, are the exact constructive certificate and do not depend on that numerical optimization.

## What arithmetic is actually needed

It is unnecessary to prove pointwise pair correlation on all of (1<|\alpha|<1.43).  It is enough to prove (4) for the matrix generated by (7), or equivalently the single integrated asymptotic (6).  On the prime side this asks for one smoothed average of prime pairs with

\[
X=T^{143/100+o(1)},\qquad |h|\lesssim X/T=T^{43/100+o(1)},
\tag{14}
\]

with the weight fixed by the autocorrelation (8).  The remaining unsupported input is therefore:

> Evaluate, or upper-bound with the required sign, the one combined off-diagonal prime-pair term in \(\operatorname{tr}(B_T^2)\) for the profile (7), strongly enough to obtain \(\operatorname{tr}(B_T^2)/N<23/20\).

This is strictly weaker as a deliverable than full Hardy--Littlewood for every shift: only the weighted average over (h\lesssim T^{0.43}) that occurs after expanding this particular Frobenius norm is needed.  All error terms in the paper that used (lambda\le1) must be recombined into this single second-moment evaluation rather than bounded separately by absolute values; otherwise the (X/T) losses erase the cancellation.

## Independent certificate: one degree-eight residual trace

There is a second route that stays at bandwidth (1) but demands higher correlations.  Let (B_T) be the normalized Hermitian matrix, (d=N+o(N)), and let

\[
q(x)=\prod_{r\in\{4/13,\,1,\,13/8,\,11/5\}}\left(1-\frac{x}{r}\right)
=1-\frac{3043}{572}x+\frac{105}{13}x^2
-\frac{2669}{572}x^3+\frac{10}{11}x^4.
\tag{15}
\]

All roots are positive and the coefficients alternate.  Hence (q(x)\ge1) for (x\le0), while (1-q(x)^2\le1) for every (x>0).  Therefore, eigenvalue by eigenvalue,

\[
\mathbf 1_{x>0}\ge1-q(x)^2,
\]

and consequently

\[
n_+(B_T)\ge d-\operatorname{tr}q(B_T)^2.
\tag{16}
\]

Combining (16) with the accepted zero-side inequality (s\ge2n_+(B_T)-N-o(N)) proves the exact implication

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}q(B_T)^2\le\left(\frac{3}{40}+o(1)\right)N
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s\ge\left(\frac{17}{20}-o(1)\right)N.}
\tag{17}
\]

This requires only one nonnegative combined observable, not eight separately stated moment theorems.  Expanding (q^2) shows that it is a fixed linear combination of (operatorname{tr}B_T^k), (0\le k\le8).  The constructive next step on this route is to compute the sine-process moments (m_5(1),\ldots,m_8(1)), minimize (int q^2\,d\mu) subject to alternating coefficients, and then attack the resulting single weighted eight-fold prime correlation directly.  Route (17) is exact, but the bound on its residual trace is not yet supplied.
